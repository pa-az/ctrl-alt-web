What is The Curiosity Gap?

Headlines and thumbnails tease just enough to open an information gap in your mind, then hide the one fact that would close it. The itch to know feels like genuine interest, but it was manufactured on purpose.

Where it comes from

Tabloids, perfected by Upworthy & BuzzFeed (2012)

How it hooks you

Exploits the information gap: curiosity spikes when we know a little but not everything, and clicking is the only relief on offer.

What the research shows

Loewenstein's information-gap theory shows curiosity peaks when a small knowledge gap is opened and only closing it brings relief, the exact recipe clickbait follows.

Source: Loewenstein, 'The Psychology of Curiosity', Psychological Bulletin (1994)

How to resist it

If a headline hides the exact thing it wants you to click for, that is the trap. Ask yourself: would I still care if I already knew the answer?
